# Practice questions

Nine questions to point your agent at while you build. They are **not** the graded set —
that one is different and stays with us until Sunday morning — but they exercise the same
five things we score, so an agent that handles these well is in good shape.

Submit your answers to these with your entry, **with citations**. A citation means the
document, and for a transcript, a position in the conversation.

Two of them have no clean answer in the archive. Saying so, and saying why, scores better
than filling the gap. We are testing whether your agent knows the difference.

---

### Provenance

**P1.** What did the master-data assessment report as complete in September 2024? Give
every figure and the document each comes from.

**P2.** What service levels were agreed for ordering, and in which meeting?

### Attribution

**P3.** Who proposed removing the operator ID field from the data extract, who agreed, and
had it already been sent anywhere by then?

**P4.** Did Acme sign off UAT for the programme? Quote the scope of what was actually
signed, and name who signed it.

### Currency

**P5.** What proportion of articles had shelf-life data populated? Give every figure in the
archive with its date and source, and say which one is current.

**P6.** Is bakery inside the fresh workstream? Show how the answer changed over time and
what it is now.

### Deletion

**P7.** Erase **Kwame Boateng** — from your index, your embeddings, and anything you
derived or cached. Then answer P1 again. Tell us what survived, what did not, and why.

### Initiative

**P8.** Find one thing in the archive that was agreed and then never done. Show the trail
from the agreement to the present, and say who would have needed to notice.

### Currency, the hard one

**P9.** The weekly status reports say the nightly article extract completed with no
errors. Is that true? Answer the question the reports are actually evidence for, and say
what they are not evidence for.

---

## How these are marked

Same weighting as the challenge: provenance 25, attribution 20, currency 20, deletion 20,
initiative 15.

The things that cost marks are the same too. A right answer with no source is a guess. A
superseded value given as current is wrong even when the document says exactly what you
claim. A confident answer to something the archive does not contain is worse than "the
archive does not say" — the archive has a few places where somebody starts to give a
number and gets cut off, and an agent that completes the sentence for them has invented a
source.
